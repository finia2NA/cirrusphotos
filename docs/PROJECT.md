# Product

This document is the source of truth for current product behavior and scope. See [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) for implementation structure and invariants.

## Purpose and constraints

SynoTimeline is a macOS 27+ SwiftUI client for browsing a very large Synology Photos library hosted on `cirrus`. Responsiveness with previously cached data matters more than minimizing request count. The application is an alpha: incompatible preferences and caches are discarded instead of migrated before 1.0.

The Synology Photos service is authoritative. The client does not poll for external changes during a session. Demanded resources are normally validated once per run, while cached content remains usable when the network is slow, unavailable, or validation fails. Personal Library, Favorites, folder and Tags pages that the run's change feeds show as unchanged count as validated without being read again.

Authentication uses `NAS_URL`, `USER` and `PW` from `.env`; `OTP` is optional for accounts without two-factor authentication. If the NAS requires it, login reports an error asking for `OTP`.

## Current product surface

The native split-view sidebar contains Library, Favorites, Recently Added, People, Subjects, Tags, Map, and Albums destinations. The app restores the last persistent destination and collection-specific photo/date scroll anchors. Session Map filters are not restored.

### Library, Favorites, and albums

The toolbar space selector restores Personal Space or Shared Space independently of albums. Shared Space provides cached-first Library, Favorites, People, Subjects, Tags and folder browsing with thumbnails, selection, Info and metadata filters, plus the common viewer, original stills, ranged video, photo downloads, favorites, photo rotation and thumbnail rotation. Shared Space Map is available; Recently Added remains unavailable. Shared Space external-editor handoff remains unavailable; its photos can be added to owned albums (see Albums). Converted video and Live Photo motion use inferred FotoTeam counterparts with validated responses; failed converted playback falls back to the original video, and unavailable motion stops safely. Recently Added has no established Shared Space endpoint and is not inferred. Albums retain their own authority and ignore the selected space. Owned albums can browse Personal and Shared Space members together, including contextual thumbnails, details and original stills. Item actions and downloads require fresh matching reads establishing one source space for the complete selection; ambiguous or mixed-space operations report an error. Album video, Live Photo motion and Shared Space album contribution remain unavailable.

The thumbnail-size slider and Cmd +/- also set the number of cards per row in the Albums overview, People, Subjects, Tags and folder grids. The Library toolbar switches between the timeline and folders in the selected space. Folder mode shows immediate child folders as a grid of cover and name cards (a folder shows up to four covers: its own, then its subfolders', then its cached photos; covers are kept across launches and recomputed once per run), with direct photos and videos below in the same scrolling browser; a separate leftmost Back button returns to the parent. When a folder has child folders but no direct photos, the photo area is blank; an empty leaf identifies the current folder as empty. Folder listings and photo metadata are cached-first and remain visible when validation fails. Folder mode and path are restored independently per space, with paths scoped to the account and server; photo scroll positions remain collection-specific. Folder browsing includes only direct photos; it does not include descendant photos.

On the NAS, sharing an owned normal album adds access settings to that same album; it remains in the normal album list. Private sharing restricts access to invitees, while public access and named-user roles are separate settings. A shared album is distinct from Synology's Shared Space. The app groups shared albums separately and shows their sharing status and named recipients. Owners can enable private sharing, invite NAS users as Viewers, Downloaders, or Providers, change or remove those grants, copy the link, and disable sharing from the album header. Owners can choose public viewing or downloading, return to private access, set or clear a write-only password, and set or clear expiry. Expiry dates end at 23:59:59 in the Mac’s current time zone. Public-link browsing uses a separate sharing context; signed-in invitees can open the same album through their regular session using its sharing reference and server-reported permissions. Incoming albums appear under Shared with me and can be browsed and pinned using their owner-scoped identity. Recipients with a verified download grant can download still photos from the album card, browser selection, or viewer; a fresh grant is required for each transfer. See the [album/sharing capture analysis](captures/ALBUM_SHARING_ANALYSIS_2026-11-01.md).

- Personal Library and Favorites share a virtualized, day-grouped fixed grid with 3–10 square slots per row, a small leading content inset, and a persisted preference for aspect-fitted or square-cropped thumbnails. The preference applies to every shared browser and does not change slot, row, or document geometry. A toolbar button beside the thumbnail-size control in each shared browser and the Library menu's **Crop Thumbnails to Square** command toggle the same preference; the button's symbol shows the resulting action (crop to square or fit to aspect ratio). Toggling animates only the thumbnail's visible frame within each fixed slot over a short ease-in-out morph, in both directions and without reissuing media work. With Reduce Motion on, the new presentation applies immediately and newly materialized rows appear directly in the current mode.
- The compact day navigator gives equal space to each populated day and appears near the pointer or with keyboard focus. A persisted option can instead keep its rail and current selection visible while idle.
- Click, Shift-click, Shift-Option-click range selection, marquee, Shift-marquee, Cmd+A, and menu-backed arrow commands select across day and page boundaries.
- Edit > Copy (Cmd+C) on a browser selection copies one `filename,id` line per item, with no header row, as text for text apps; fields containing commas or quotes are CSV-quoted. Unloaded selected items are resolved first. The same text is offered beside the file when dragging out of the browser or the viewer, so text fields accept it while Finder and Photos take the files; it is offered only when every selected photo is already loaded. Albums do not offer it. Pasting files into Finder or Photos is not supported yet; use drag and drop.
- Favorites appear as heart badges. The toolbar and Library menu `.` command toggle the active viewer photo or browser selection, with confirmation when more than five items are affected or the entire collection is selected.
- Library menu **Delete…** (Command-Delete) and the browser context menu permanently delete the selected photos and videos from Personal Space, Shared Space, or an owned album's verified members. A confirmation sheet warns before anything is sent; after the NAS finishes, the app reads the items back, reports any that remain, and reloads all collections. Deleting from an album deletes the item everywhere, unlike **Remove from Album…**. Deletion from the viewer, mixed Personal/Shared selections, and incoming albums are unavailable.
- The browser context menu, the viewer context menu and the Library menu set a **Rating** of zero to five stars (the Library menu uses the digit keys 0–5), **Add Tag** (an existing tag, or **New Tag…**, which creates the tag if the name is new) and **Remove Tag**. They act on the viewer photo or the browser selection in Personal Space, Shared Space and owned albums; tags are listed from the item's own space, so albums offer only **New Tag…**. Edits are not optimistic: the app writes, reads the photos back, and updates the cached rating and tags and the open browser and viewer; the Tags index and the collections of changed tags are read again. Incoming albums and mixed-space selections are unavailable. Tags are not renamed or deleted; a tag disappears when its last photo loses it.
- Toolbar controls rotate the active viewer photo or the current browser selection left or right. Rotation is optimistic, supports photos and Live Photo stills, and adopts refreshed orientation, resolution, and thumbnail descriptors from the NAS; videos are skipped.
- Right-clicking a photo in any shared browser or the viewer offers original and compressed JPEG downloads, either to the configured folder or a folder chosen for that download. Right-clicking a selected photo downloads the selected still photos; right-clicking another photo selects it. Videos are skipped. The configured folder defaults to the macOS Downloads folder. A single ordinary photo's original can reuse cached full-resolution bytes; compressed JPEG, Live Photo, and multi-item downloads use the server response, a ZIP that is unzipped and saved as a folder of files named after the archive (for example `download`), numbered if it exists.
- The same menus offer **Edit With** for the photo under the pointer, even when multiple items are selected. macOS lists applications registered for its file type with their icons and provides an **Other…** chooser. The app creates a separate original copy in `Documents/SynoTimeline Edits`, keeps existing copies, and opens the copy in the chosen application. It can reuse a cached full-resolution still, including for a Live Photo. Editor changes stay in the local working copy or wherever the editor saves them; they are not uploaded to the NAS. RAW files indexed as Photos items use the same path. Discovering separate, unindexed RAW or sidecar files is not implemented.
- Dragging a photo from a shared browser or the viewer to a file-promise destination copies the original on drop. Dragging a browser selection of several photos produces one folder of the unzipped originals, since a file promise delivers one item. The preview is assembled from at most 20 available thumbnails with a selection-count badge. Drag previews follow the thumbnail shape setting: centered square crops or native aspect ratios. Videos are skipped. A Live Photo dragged individually provides its still image. Downloading starts after the destination accepts the drop; progress and failures appear in Downloads. At viewer fit zoom, dragging the image starts an external drag; at higher zoom, dragging pans. Marquee selection starts on empty browser space; Shift modifies that marquee selection.
- Albums shows Albums, Shared Albums, and Shared with me in that order, each initially limited to one responsive row with independently persisted expansion. A persisted sort orders each section by name (default), newest, or oldest creation time. The toolbar’s plus button creates an empty album. Cards show cover, name, and item count. Owned album cards offer the four photo download commands for the complete album without opening it; videos are skipped and an empty or video-only album reports that no eligible photos are available. Opening an album reuses the shared browser with collection-scoped restoration and a scrolling cover, name, full-album capture-date range, sharing status, and recipient avatars with hover names, including while loading or when empty or filtered; Escape returns from an opened album or map result just as its Back button does.
- Drag a Personal or Shared Space browser selection onto an owned album card or pinned row to add its selected photos and videos, or, for Personal Space selections only, onto a Shared with me album when the recipient has Provider access; hover over Albums in the sidebar to open the overview during a drag. Existing members are skipped for owned destinations. The source collection is unchanged. Drag an owned album card or pinned row to a file-promise destination to export its eligible original photos as an album-named folder. Drag a card to the sidebar to pin it at an insertion point, or drag a pin to reorder the pins. Incoming album cards can be pinned and reordered in the sidebar, but do not offer file export. Incoming albums show cached or fetched thumbnails; original images, video playback, Live Photo motion, item download and edit, favorites, rotation, and adding their items to albums are unavailable. Internal drops do not start a download.
- Drag local image files from Finder onto a Personal or Shared Space library timeline, onto the folder shown in Personal Space folder view, or onto an owned album's browser, card, or sidebar pin, to upload them. Folder drops upload into that exact folder, with no date subfolders, and the Uploads toolbar names it. Uploads preserve the local files and use the NAS's ignore-duplicate behavior; an existing item can still be added to an album. The Uploads toolbar shows progress, cancellation, and per-file errors. A successful or uncertain upload refreshes the affected library and album through cached-first metadata reconciliation; check the destination before retrying an uncertain transfer. Thumbnails of uploaded items may appear up to about a minute later, while the NAS generates them; the app checks for a bounded time and stops when you leave the browser. Incoming albums, videos, and watched-folder uploads are not supported. Shared Space folder uploads use the Personal contract but have not been verified against the NAS.
- Album cards offer **Add to Sidebar** and **Delete Album…**. Pinned albums appear below Albums with a cover and name, open the same browser and saved position as their cards, and offer **Remove from Sidebar**, **Delete Album…**, and the same four album download commands. Deleting a normal owned album requires confirmation and does not delete its photos or videos. Pins persist per account and NAS, remain available offline, and are removed only when a verified complete album list no longer contains them. Back or Escape returns to the Albums overview.
- The Library menu offers **New Album…** and **Add to Album…** for the current browser selection, including photos and videos. Photo and video context menus also offer **Add to Album**, including **New Album…** and eligible sidebar pins first, and **Remove from Album…** in normal albums. The viewer acts on its current item; the browser uses the right-click selection. The add chooser lists albums the server reports as addable. **Remove from Album…** is available in an opened album and its filtered results; it removes membership without deleting library items. Operations capture and verify their selection before writing, and remain visible during navigation. Owned normal albums can be renamed from album cards and sidebar pins. Shared Space selections use the same commands and menus; contributing them to Shared with me albums remains unavailable.
- Personal Library, Favorites, opened Personal Space folders and albums, incoming albums, and Map pin or cluster results have a session-only metadata filter for filename, media kind, camera, lens, literal file extension, tags, star rating, megapixels, size, ISO, focal length, aperture, and shutter speed. Selected tags match photos with any of them; rating is any, unrated, or at least one to five stars. Both use cached metadata like the other criteria, so no server request is made. Camera, lens, and extension options are cached per persistent collection and shown while background discovery runs, with a loading indicator. Previously known options remain available when cached page coverage is incomplete. Map result options are cached for the lifetime of that session collection. Applying a filter immediately shows matches from metadata already indexed. Background discovery adds other cached pages from the current timeline, including noncontiguous older pages, to results and option catalogs. Pages cached later by browsing or either warmer update the filter catalog and active results through the repository. The filter is available from a cached collection before the server connects; background expansion starts once the collection is validated. Filtered browsers show available cached matches before network validation finishes and retain them when validation fails. Results expand as the app-wide background metadata sync and browsing store pages; scrolling near the edge of the results requests nearby source sections first. Failed sections remain available for explicit retry. Filter criteria and coverage are cleared when changing the source collection and are not restored after relaunch. Map filtering applies only within the selected pin or cluster. Filename search matches a case- and diacritic-insensitive substring; the `.*` button in the field switches to a case-insensitive regular expression matched anywhere in the filename. ### People

People lists visible recognized people, including the server's expanded list, with face covers and item counts. Opening a person reuses the shared browser, viewer, selection and metadata filters. The index and opened timelines are cached-first and remain available when validation fails. Back or Escape returns to People. A person card’s Rename command changes their name. As a name is entered, matching named people in the same space appear with face thumbnails; choosing one selects them as a merge target. Merging asks for confirmation; the target identity is retained. A change is confirmed by refreshing the server index, and an uncertain outcome is reported for review before retrying.

### Map

Map shows geotagged photos from the selected Personal or Shared Space library as thumbnail pins and counted clusters. Dense areas group more coarsely to limit the map to 64 annotations; each cluster retains its photos. Opening a pin or cluster creates a session-only filtered collection and reuses the shared browser. Cached canonical item details are shown in their exact ID slots before network validation. The camera remains useful across metadata invalidation; stale cached pins remain visible when validation fails, and verification is retried automatically.

Shared Space keeps its own full GPS inventory, updated by the Shared Space Diff feed like Personal Space; a full scan with bounded reads runs only when that inventory is incomplete. A failed or inconsistent scan retains the preceding cached pins.

Location labels and antimeridian-specific clustering are not implemented.

### Viewer and Info panel

The viewer can show a resizable filmstrip of nearby items, a draggable histogram, and red highlight peaking for near-neutral bright pixels in the rendered photo. A pixel qualifies when its brightest sRGB channel meets the threshold and its HSV saturation is at most 5%; for example, (244, 243, 255) qualifies at 100%, but saturated red does not. The 5% neutral-color tolerance is a practical heuristic rather than a universal clipping standard. Settings controls the brightness threshold from 80% to 100%, defaulting to 100%; changes apply immediately and persist. This is a rendered-image white highlight indicator, not a raw sensor clipping detector. The filmstrip follows the library thumbnail shape setting and displays favorite and video duration badges. Clicking the histogram switches between luminance and additively blended RGB channels; this choice persists across viewer sessions and app launches. The histogram stays below the toolbar, animates to a corner using drag momentum on release, and fades with the viewer backdrop during dismissal. Focus edges marks in green, as one-pixel-wide lines along gradient ridges that continue for at least about ten pixels (isolated dots and fine texture such as rug pile or noise are not marked), where the photo's edges are sharpest. Each edge pixel's width (local contrast divided by gradient slope) is measured at up to 20 megapixels, and pixels up to a settable multiple of the image's own sharpest edges (10th percentile) are marked. Settings controls that multiple from 1× to 2.5×, defaulting to 1.5×. The result is relative to the photo: it shows where focus is best, not whether the photo is sharp, so a uniformly soft photo still marks its crispest edges. Noise, JPEG artifacts, in-camera sharpening, and high-contrast out-of-focus edges can mislead it, and flat areas are never marked. The toolbar and View menu toggle each tool. The histogram samples the currently displayed photo; peaking checks up to four samples per overlay pixel after bounded downscaling and shows their coverage; small highlights can be missed or blended away. Both update as the displayed image changes; videos do not show these inspection tools.

Space or double-click opens a photo or ordinary video in the same window. Photos support arrow navigation, zoom, pan, thumbnail-first loading, cached large previews, and originals. A mouse wheel zooms around the pointer, with upward scrolling zooming in and downward scrolling zooming out; the zoom control reflects the change. Configured thumbnails for up to two photos in each direction and, when enabled and available, large previews for immediate neighbors are fetched and decoded ahead of navigation, so a cached next photo can appear without a blank frame or loading indicator. At fit zoom, a horizontal two-finger trackpad swipe pages interactively between adjacent items: both items follow the fingers and release past a threshold snaps to the neighbor; above fit zoom horizontal scrolling pans instead. Videos retain the thumbnail as a poster until playback begins, provide a toolbar mute control synchronized with native controls and remembered for future videos, use native playback controls, and deliver authenticated video bytes progressively without first downloading the whole file. Cached segments can be reused for later playback. Arrow navigation remains previous/next and Space, Escape, or Back closes the viewer. Filename, capture-local date, favorite mutation, and the Info panel remain available for both media types. Live Photos display an image-corner indicator. After the full still loads, clicking it plays the motion once, while a Force Touch press plays until released; a loading indicator appears until the first frame is ready. Motion plays without controls and returns to the still afterward. Motion is unavailable if member lookup fails or the still has been rotated on the NAS.

Still photos open from the clicked thumbnail and close back into the current item's live thumbnail. Both transitions are compositor-backed, so a large preview or original upgrade never makes them stutter. The viewer background is white in light appearance and black in dark appearance. At fit zoom a predominantly downward two-finger trackpad swipe dismisses the current item the way Apple Photos does: the photo follows the gesture, scales down subtly, and the backdrop fades to reveal the library. Releasing after enough downward distance or speed sends the photo into its current thumbnail; releasing below that threshold returns it smoothly to full screen. Two-finger scrolling pans whenever the photo is zoomed, and click-drag never dismisses. Reduce Motion replaces the spatial dismissal with a short fade. Trackpad swipes over videos navigate or dismiss instead of scrubbing; native controls still provide seeking.

Settings provides Automatic, Original, High, Medium, Low, and Mobile default video quality choices. Automatic targets High. For a converted choice, the app uses only renditions advertised by the current item; if the requested quality is absent, it chooses the closest advertised quality and prefers the lower one when equally distant. If no converted rendition is advertised, it streams the stored original. The toolbar reports the effective rendition and identifies an unavailable requested quality when fallback occurs.

Settings also controls pixel interpolation in the photo viewer. **Always** smooths every displayed image, **Thumbnails Only** smooths provisional thumbnails and large previews until the original is displayed, and **Off** disables smoothing. The preference updates an open viewer immediately.

The permanent Downloads toolbar button is filled while jobs are active and opens a list of download jobs, with progress when the server supplies a length and a cancel action while active. Single-photo jobs use the filename; multiple-photo jobs use their capture-date range. Failed jobs remain visible. Closing the main window or quitting while jobs run asks before cancelling them. Downloads first go to temporary storage and only completed files are placed in the destination; existing files are never overwritten.

The Cmd+I Info utility panel follows the active viewer item or browser selection. It shows selection totals and date range, resolves aggregate size and media counts, and shows a single item's media-kind icon, filename, copyable item ID and available Synology folder path, capture date, a five-star rating (click a star to set it, or the current star to clear it) and rounded tag chips, camera/exposure details, dimensions, size, format, and available location context. Its location map includes every located item in the selection and refits to the pins when the selection changes. It is transient and excluded from Mission Control and the normal window cycle.

## Cache and network behavior

- Timelines, exact pages, albums, location records, thumbnails, previews, originals, and previously fetched video ranges are delivered cached-first.
- One repository-owned metadata lifecycle coordinates admission, shared jobs, stale-result rejection, storage generations, atomic same-row commits, and successful invalidation revisions.
- **Refresh** (toolbar icon, Library ▸ Refresh from NAS, ⌘R) reads each space's Diff v7 feed now and withdraws this run's validation of collection catalogs and album, folder and summary lists, so they are read from the NAS again. Nothing is cleared: open views keep their content and merge the changes in. With a failed sign-in or an unloaded library it restarts loading instead. Nothing is polled automatically.
- **Revalidate Metadata** in Settings keeps cached metadata visible but withdraws its verification and the Diff cursor, so every timeline, section and detail is read from the NAS again on demand and by the background sync. It is the escape hatch for metadata the change feeds missed.
- A metadata-only Settings reset removes timelines, pages, album metadata, locations, and session filters while preserving compressed media, validators, decoded thumbnails, and already admitted media transfers. Full clear removes metadata and media.
- One repository-owned engine serves all collections. Bounded terminal Diff v7 intervals (the item feed plus the favorite events from `list_user_event`) reconcile canonical content, favorite flags and compact Map coverage across runs, and prove untouched cached Library, folder and Tags pages current in both spaces, plus Personal Favorites; folder moves, tag and rating edits arrive as item changes. Shared Space has its own feed, assumed to behave like Personal Space, but no favorite-event feed. Albums, People, Subjects, Recently Added, incoming albums and Shared Favorites still require authoritative reads per run. Unsupported or ambiguous intervals recover conservatively.
- Logical metadata pages contain at most 250 items from one day. Network batches combine adjacent demand, so small days do not each require a request. Evicted details leave known IDs and positions intact; available cached slots remain usable offline.
- Thumbnail rendition changes preserve other cached sizes. Compatible fallback renditions remain visible until the selected rendition is available.
- A failed thumbnail request offers a retry control on that photo; any cached fallback stays visible while retrying.
- An optional viewer setting loads cached or network-backed `xl` previews alongside originals when the library rendition is smaller.
- The Library menu can warm the selected thumbnail rendition for an inclusive populated-day range using bounded, cancellable background work.
- While the app runs, a background metadata sync reads every page of Personal Library, Favorites, every owned normal album and every folder (plus Shared Library, Shared Favorites and Shared Space folders when **Include Shared Space** is on) that is not yet verified this run, at lower priority than visible work. The collection on screen is loaded first. Sections that fail are retried automatically with increasing delays, and immediately when the network returns, until everything is loaded. The Filter popover shows this progress for its own timeline.
- **Load All Timeline Sections** is an app-wide metadata warmer for Personal Library, Favorites, and every owned normal album. **Include Shared Space** adds Shared Library and Shared Favorites; this preference defaults on and is shared with the thumbnail warming window. Pressing Start discovers the album list and unopened album timelines, then loads all of their item pages; Map/location indexing remains separate. By default it bypasses stored pages and validates every section; **Use Cached Pages** skips complete cached pages and repairs missing detail slots. It does not download thumbnail bytes.
- **Download Thumbnails** warms Personal Library, optionally including Shared Library through the same **Include Shared Space** preference. Its date slider combines both libraries’ populated dates when enabled; the selected inclusive calendar interval applies independently to each library. Each job captures its scope when Start is pressed and retains the existing bounded page/thumbnail work and cache budget.
- Demanded video bytes are persisted in fixed-size segments under the same disk budget. They have lower retention priority than every photo rendition, use a separate two-transfer lane, and are never prefetched for adjacent videos. A partially cached video is not presented as fully available offline.
- Settings controls thumbnail rendition, large viewer previews, default video quality, and a 200 MB–100 GB disk budget.

## Mutations and freshness

Favorite writes are optimistic and serialized. Pending values are presentation overlays; only confirmed chunks patch canonical favorite fields. Failure removes the overlay, and successful writes refresh authoritative Favorites membership. Rotation patches only its owned fields, preserving concurrent favorite changes. A Favorites viewer preserves its opening playlist while membership changes.

Normal photo rotation is available in the toolbar for the active viewer item or browser selection. The native Edit menu contains icon-labeled left/right commands for both normal and thumbnail-only rotation plus “Fix Thumbnail Rotations…”. Normal rotation changes Synology's display orientation and generated renditions. Thumbnail-only rotation runs in a modal, cancellable progress workflow: it downloads `sm`, `m`, and `xl`, rotates JPEG bytes locally, replaces only those server renditions through the regeneration workflow, refreshes descriptors, and leaves original bytes plus orientation/resolution metadata unchanged. Its smoothly animated per-photo progress bar assigns one half to downloads, one sixth to local work, and one third to upload. The download half is weighted by estimated thumbnail sizes and the original's known filesize, uses live byte progress for the full photo, and names the active small, medium, large, or full-photo transfer. The scrollable result log contains one outcome per photo: the applied rotation, already correct, or failure reason. Automatic repair also obtains the full image, uses ImageIO to compare all four relative rotations of each rendition against its intended display orientation, and uploads only when `sm`, `m`, and `xl` independently make the same confident choice. Already-correct, ambiguous, undecodable, missing, or disagreeing renditions are not mutated. Post-regeneration failure or cancellation restores each affected thumbnail unit out of regenerating state. Shared Space uses inferred FotoTeam regeneration and upload counterparts; their write behavior has not yet been verified on a disposable fixture.

Photo capture times are treated as capture-local wall-clock values. Grouping and display preserve the encoded local time without inferring a timezone from the Mac or GPS data.

## Deferred scope

Generated people/subject albums, place names, watched-folder upload, and coordinated session renewal after expiry remain future work. New collection types should reuse the repository and shared browser rather than introduce destination-specific cache systems.

## Subjects

Subjects lists Synology’s recognized categories with their cover and item count. Opening a subject shows its photos in the shared browser, with the usual selection, viewer, and session metadata filters. The index and opened collections are cached-first and remain available when validation fails. Subject management is not implemented.

## Tags

Tags lists Synology tags with their cover and item count. Opening a tag shows its photos in the shared browser, with selection, viewer and session metadata filters. The index and opened collections are cached-first and remain available when validation fails. Tags are assigned and removed from photos (see above); to find photos by tag within a library, favorites, folder or album, use the Tags and Rating criteria of the filter instead of opening the tag.

### Recently Added

Recently Added shows the NAS collection in the shared browser, grouped by indexing day and ordered by the server. Capture dates remain available in photo metadata. Its timeline and requested pages are cached-first and remain available when validation fails. Membership is determined by the NAS; it is not calculated from a client-side age threshold.
